# Prompts and Intentional Capture

## Confirmed

Story Prompts gently encourage media and reflection that will matter later. The goal is better intentional media, not indiscriminately more media.

Confirmed examples include taking one photo together, recording ten seconds of motion, capturing sound or feeling, photographing morning coffee or a first bite, and recording a brief moment before leaving.

## Pre-departure preparation

“Prepare Your Story” is the working name for a future pre-departure experience; final customer-facing wording remains **TBD**. It should teach travelers to capture better story material without turning travel into a production. The goal is not more media, but better and more intentional media.

Concise guidance may encourage travelers to:

- photograph people as well as scenery and ensure the travelers themselves appear;
- preserve candid interaction, natural laughter, and conversation alongside posed images;
- capture one or two meaningful images at important stops rather than documenting everything;
- photograph transitions and small details;
- record short, intentional video clips, including ten seconds of motion, a first reaction, a moment before leaving, or both travelers together;
- capture the sound of a place when it matters;
- use Story Moments and Campfire when something genuinely matters.

Video is memory, not social-media content. Useful clips may later connect to Living Album pages through QR codes or other branded media markers.

> The scenery shows where we traveled. The people show why we remember it.

Near departure, Storied Journey should eventually provide a short, personalized Story Prep—for example, five to seven things worth capturing rather than an instruction to document everything. Suggestions may use the actual itinerary, key reservations, important destinations, Story Prompts, known visual opportunities, and meaningful people or heritage context. Exact recommendation logic remains **TBD**.

Journey-specific preparation may include weather, footwear, driving conventions, connectivity and offline readiness, power adapters, reservation timing, airport or transit considerations, photography restrictions, drone rules, environmental sensitivities, accessibility, and comfort when relevant. Concise cultural guidance may address photographing people, private property, religious sites, dress, tipping and customs, conversation, sensitive locations, drone etiquette, and respect for local communities.

The tone should be practical and respectful, guided by this principle: **Be a traveler participating in the place, not an observer collecting people and locations.**

Apply this inclusion filter: **Will knowing this before departure meaningfully improve the journey, prevent a problem, or help preserve the story?** If not, it probably does not belong.

Preparation should be framed as “Capture the moments you'll be glad you preserved,” never as taking more photos in order to buy a book. A better Album is a consequence of helping the traveler experience and remember the journey well.
