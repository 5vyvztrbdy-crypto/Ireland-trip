# Opportunity Meter and Unexpected Joys

## Confirmed

The Opportunity Meter uses positive framing to help travelers understand where flexibility creates opportunity. The primary exploration action is provisionally described as “Explore Nearby” or “What's Nearby?”; final traveler-facing wording remains **TBD**. It is more than a conventional Near Me search.

Recommendations must follow the No Surprise Rule: explain why the opportunity is shown. The eventual recommendation model should consider current location, next destination, route, protected reservation anchors, available time, traveler preferences, reasonable detour, and Journey Perspective / Opportunity Meter context. The exact model remains **TBD**.

“Unexpected Joy” is not the exploration button. It describes a prompted or unprompted worthwhile discovery—such as a place, overlook, or short sightseeing stop—that the traveler chooses to preserve after experiencing it. A preserved Unexpected Joy can become a Story Moment, a Campfire memory, media or story material, and potential Storied Album content.

Preserve the Pause concept while evaluating “Take Your Time” as potentially better traveler-facing language. This action expresses an intentional choice to slow down. Storied Journey should reconsider the flexible parts of the day's plan without making the traveler feel late or behind.

Explore Nearby and Pause / Take Your Time belong primarily in Today / Journey Perspective, not prominently on the Reservations screen.

## Current implementation

“Surprise Me” randomly chooses a generic category and opens a Google Maps search. It explicitly states that it does not search live businesses. This is a demonstration and has been retired as the primary exploration concept; it is not the confirmed capability.
