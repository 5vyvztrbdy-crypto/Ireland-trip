# Experience Lifecycle

## Confirmed

Plan → Reservations → Prepare Your Story → Journey Perspective → Travel → Unexpected Joys → Story Prompts → Photos/Video → Story Moments → Campfire → Rough First Draft Storied Album → Traveler Editing → Digital Publishing → Physical Publishing → Living Album Experience → Next Journey.

“Prepare Your Story” is the working name for a concise pre-departure experience; final customer-facing wording remains **TBD**. It helps travelers prepare for both the journey and the story they may want to preserve afterward. It is not a general destination guide. Guidance belongs only when it can meaningfully improve the journey, prevent an avoidable problem, help preserve the story, or improve the eventual Storied Album.

At first launch, a cached sample Storied Album demonstrates the eventual result and value proposition. It uses a demonstration journey such as Joyce Journey and is not generated from the new traveler’s trip.

At journey completion, the invitation is: “Let’s start building your Storied Album.” The system produces a rough first draft, not an allegedly perfect finished product. The traveler then refines the story as author, with AI as an editorial and design assistant.

## Sprint 2 authorized information architecture

Primary application navigation is **Home / Journey / Memories**. Reservations remain canonical working capability within Plan & Journey rather than a permanent top-level destination. Heritage and research information remains journey data to be surfaced in Journey and future Storied Perspective context, not a standalone feature.

**Memories** is the durable collection of captured journey material and meaning. **Campfire** is a small persistent capture ritual that brings today's experiences into that collection. The product flow is **Campfire → Memories → Storied Album**.

The shared local journey-preference foundation records multiple interests and one detour tolerance. Surprise consumes it in Slice 1; future Discover Nearby, Storied Perspective, and “What am I about to miss?” capabilities should consume the same preference source rather than create separate settings.
